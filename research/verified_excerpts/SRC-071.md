# SRC-071 — "God became man so that man might become god" (heard as Irenaeus; classic source Athanasius, De Incarnatione 54.3)

- **Heard wording** (first-retreat transcript, evangelization talk; mine T6-S15; raw ASR): "Not one of your early Christian saints Saint. Inaus say that God became man, so that man may become God." (ASR "Inaus" garbled — pipeline suspects Irenaeus; the maxim as worded is classically Athanasius's. Context: the speaker's answer to the objection "don't you Christians believe that you will become like God?" in the Mormonism discussion.)

## Authoritative excerpts (verbatim from fetched pages)
Athanasius, De Incarnatione 54.3 (Robertson, New Advent — note New Advent's space before the semicolon):
"For He was made man that we might be made God ; and He manifested Himself by a body that we might receive the idea of the unseen Father; and He endured the insolence of men that we might inherit immortality."

CCC ¶460 (official English, vatican.va — with its footnotes):
"460 The Word became flesh to make us "partakers of the divine nature":78 "For this is why the Word became man, and the Son of God became the Son of man: so that man, by entering into communion with the Word and thus receiving divine sonship, might become a son of God."79 "For the Son of God became man so that we might become God."80 "The only-begotten Son of God, wanting to make us sharers in his divinity, assumed our nature, so that he, made man, might make men gods."81"
Footnotes: "79 St. Irenaeus, Adv. haeres. 3, 19, 1: PG 7/1, 939." / "80 St. Athanasius, De inc. 54, 3: PG 25, 192B." / "81 St. Thomas Aquinas, Opusc. 57, 1-4."

Irenaeus, Adversus Haereses V, praef. (ANF, New Advent):
"…but following the only true and steadfast Teacher, the Word of God, our Lord Jesus Christ, who did, through His transcendent love, become what we are, that He might bring us to be even what He is Himself."

## Comparison notes
- Heard: "God became man, so that man may become God." — a near-verbatim match to the CCC ¶460 rendering of Athanasius ("For the Son of God became man so that we might become God"), differing only in the opening words and pronoun/mood. Robertson's older rendering ("For He was made man that we might be made God") is the same maxim.
- Attribution nuance: the maxim as worded is classically Athanasius's (De Incarnatione 54.3; PG 25, 192B), which is how the CCC quotes it (¶460, fn. 80). Irenaeus carries a genuine parallel formula (Adv. Haer. V, praef. — and the CCC also quotes a different Irenaeus deification text at ¶460, fn. 79: Adv. haeres. 3, 19, 1: PG 7/1, 939), so an Irenaeus attribution is not indefensible — but "Saint Inaus" cannot be resolved from the ASR alone (human audio check recommended; the mine's note says "the maxim is classically A[thanasius]…" and lists both candidates).
- Recommendation for the book: the safe citation is Athanasius, On the Incarnation of the Word 54.3 (PG 25, 192B), as quoted in CCC ¶460 ("For the Son of God became man so that we might become God"); mention Irenaeus's parallel (Adv. Haer. V praef. and III.19.1) in a note if the speaker's wording is kept. Do not print the heard attribution name until a human re-listen confirms what was said.
