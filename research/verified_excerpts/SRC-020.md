## Heard wording (clean transcript)
As Jacques Maritain said — he was a philosopher, a Thomist philosopher in the 20th century — mysteries are pregnant with intelligibility. They are so full of things we can know.

## Evidence — verbatim from fetched sources
And being is a mystery, either because it is too pregnant with intelligibility, too pure for our intellect which is the case with spiritual things, or because its nature presents a more or less impenetrable ... (sentence end not captured in OCR fragments)

## Discrepancy / comparison notes
Speaker compresses Maritain's sentence ('being is a mystery, either because it is too pregnant with intelligibility...') into 'mysteries are pregnant with intelligibility.' Phrase confirmed verbatim in 1939 edition OCR (two scans). Checked and NOT found in The Degrees of Knowledge (1937 OCR + 1946 French 5th ed. — no 'pregnant', no 'superintelligible'). Page ~172 is a scan-index reading — human check of a print copy recommended.
