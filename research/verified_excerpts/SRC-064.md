# SRC-064 — Flannery O'Connor, "Dogma is the guardian of mystery"

- **Heard wording** (first-retreat transcript, post-Mystery Q&A; mine T3-4; raw ASR): "Dogma, which is official church teaching, dogma is the guardian of mystery. Plenary O Connor said that." (ASR "Plenary" = Flannery; mine restores "Flannery O'Connor said that.")

- **Edition/translation:** first edition, Farrar, Straus and Giroux, New York, 1979

## Authoritative excerpt (OCR of the 1979 edition, search-inside snippet — sentence copied verbatim)
"Dogma is the guardian of mystery. The doctrines are spiritually significant in ways that we cannot fathom."

(Snippet context, stitched from adjacent search-inside windows of the same letter — read with caution, not page-verified as continuous text: "…invisible elevator into the clouds. We don't know how the Assumption or the Immaculate Conception were brought about nor is this a matter for science in any way. Dogma is the guardian of mystery. The doctrines are spiritually significant in ways that we cannot fathom.")

## Comparison notes
- Heard sentence "Dogma is the guardian of mystery" matches O'Connor verbatim (same words, same punctuation of the sentence core).
- Source correction for the mine (talks34 T3-4 note): the phrase is NOT from the 1957 essay "The Church and the Fiction Writer" — a search-inside negative check of *Mystery and Manners* (FSG 1969, indexed in Open Library) found no "guardian of mystery" hit. The verified source is the letter to Cecil Dawkins, 23 December 1959, in *The Habit of Being*.
- Secondary confirmations of the letter attribution (fetched via search-inside): McMullen, *Writing Against God* (Mercer Univ. Press, 1995): "in a 1959 letter to Cecil Dawkins: Dogma is the guardian of mystery. The doctrines are spiritually significant…"; Ellsberg (ed.), *Flannery O'Connor: Spiritual Writings* (Orbis, 2003): "She writes to Cecil Dawkins, another good friend, on December 23, 1959…".
- Recommendation: cite as Flannery O'Connor, letter to Cecil Dawkins, 23 December 1959, in *The Habit of Being*, ed. Sally Fitzgerald (New York: Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 1979). Print-copy page check recommended before a page cite.
