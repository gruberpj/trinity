## Heard wording (clean transcript)
To hope is to affirm being, says Gabriel Marcel. To despair is to negate being, to give up on all existence.

## Evidence — verbatim from fetched sources
Being and Having (Dacre 1949), Metaphysical Diary, Feb. 1931: At the root of the hope is the consciousness of a state of things which invites us to despair (illness, damnation, etc.). To hope is to put one's trust in reality, to assert that it contains the means of triumphing over this danger... 22 Mar. 1931: To despair of a man is not this to deny him as a soul? (also, 'On the Ontological Mystery': ...to deny that an unconditional affirmation of Being is possible...)

## Discrepancy / comparison notes
Heard formulation ('affirm being / negate being') is the speaker's summary of Marcel's hope/despair dialectic — not located verbatim; the fetched diary passages corroborate the idea. Marcel's fullest treatment is Homo Viator: Introduction to a Metaphysic of Hope (1951) — not fetchable (lending). Print check recommended if the book cites Marcel on hope.
