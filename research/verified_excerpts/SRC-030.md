## Heard wording (clean transcript)
E01: of living in what Max Weber, a 19th-century German philosopher, called 'living in an enchanted world that has a magic behind it.' C03: Max Weber, a German philosopher of the 19th century, said, 'This [possibly: It] is an enchanted world.' ... the material world is brimming with the invisible.

## Evidence — verbatim from fetched sources
German (1919 pamphlet, p. 16): Das aber bedeutet: die Entzauberung der Welt. Nicht mehr, wie der Wilde, für den es solche Mächte gab, muß man zu magischen Mitteln greifen, um die Geister zu beherrschen oder zu erbitten. English (Gerth & Mills, p. 155): The fate of our times is characterized by rationalization and intellectualization and, above all, by the 'disenchantment of the world.' (p. 139: This means that 'the world is disenchanted.')

## Discrepancy / comparison notes
The speaker INVOKES Weber's famous 'disenchantment of the world' (Entzauberung der Welt) but presents its inversion ('an enchanted world... that has a magic behind it') — that wording is not Weber's. Also: Weber (1864-1920) is usually classed as an early-20th-century sociologist; the speaker says 'a 19th-century German philosopher' (kept as heard; author may wish to adjust). The German sentence and the standard English translation are both verified.
