# SRC-045 — Verification Pass 2 (fresh angles) — 2026-09-16

## Heard wording (exact, from epilogue draft line ~127 / transcript anchor E01 @ 00:30:15–00:30:21)
> Every year on the feast day of St. Augustine … you will find some Catholic person likes to quote this one thing that St. Augustine apparently said: "I looked into my deepest wound, and there I saw your glory, and it dazzled me." [quotation wording unverified]

Common circulating form of the meme: "In my deepest wound I saw your glory, and it dazzled me."

## Verdict
**NOT_FOUND as a verbatim sentence of Augustine (Latin or any translation).** The exact English wording appears in no edition/translation of Augustine and in no pseudo-Augustine work searched. **NEW since Stage 2: a genuine proximate source has been identified** — the sentence is a modern devotional paraphrase of *Confessions* X.41.66, which is very likely the real passage behind the meme:

- Latin (Conf. X.41.66): *"vidi enim splendorem tuum corde saucio et repercussus dixi, 'quis illuc potest?' proiectus sum a facie oculorum tuorum."* — The Latin Library: https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/augustine/conf10.shtml
- English (Pusey, ch. 41 §66): "For with a wounded heart have I seen Your brightness, and being beaten back I exclaimed, 'Who can attain unto it?' I am cut off from before Your eyes." — New Advent: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110110.htm

Mapping of the paraphrase: *splendorem tuum* → "your glory"; *corde saucio* ("with wounded heart") → "my deepest wound"; *repercussus* ("struck/beaten back" — i.e., dazzled by the light) → "it dazzled me". The r/latin thread (Jan 2024, reddit.com/r/latin/comments/1af20iz) independently reaches the same conclusion ("in my copy … book X chapter 41 … 'For in my wounded heart, I saw your splendor, and it dazzled me'"). A commenter in that thread renders *repercussus* as "it dazzled me," matching the meme wording.

## Recommended book treatment
Keep the speaker's hedge exactly as heard ("apparently said") — it is factually correct: Augustine never wrote this sentence, and no verified translation contains it. Do **not** remove the hedge or present it as a verbatim Augustine quotation. Best-practice options for the book:
1. (Minimal) Keep the hedge; label in endnotes: *traditional/unverified wording — modern paraphrase of Confessions X.41.66*.
2. (Preferred) Keep the hedge and add an endnote citing the genuine seed passage: "the closest genuine text is Confessions X.41.66: 'with a wounded heart have I seen Your brightness, and being beaten back I exclaimed, Who can attain unto it?' (Latin: 'vidi enim splendorem tuum corde saucio et repercussus dixi: quis illuc potest?')."
Do not attribute the sentence to Rohr or any specific modern author without further verification of *Everything Belongs* p. 166.
