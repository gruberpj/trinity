## 1. Problem = "notional complex… disentangled" — VERIFIED_EXACT

- **Paper wording:** "the 'problem' may be described as a notional complex created by our intellect, which at first appears inextricable and which must be disentangled"
- **Published (First Lecture, §5, p. 4 in the 1948 printing; Mentor p. 13):** "The 'problem' may be described as a notional complex created by our intellect, which at first appears inextricable and which must be disentangled. I am speaking of the problem in its pure state. You will soon see that there are other cases in which the 'problem' aspect reappears, but no longer isolated, in combination with the 'mystery' aspect."
- **Comparison:** verbatim.

## 2. "adhesion of the mind… growing intimacy" — VERIFIED_EXACT

- **Paper wording:** "its progress is progress by an adhesion of the mind to its object and a union with it increasingly profound, progress as it were by a growing intimacy"
- **Published (First Lecture, §6, p. 8 in the 1948 printing):** "But progress of this kind is not the law of wisdom. Its progress is progress by an adhesion of the mind to its object and a union with it increasingly profound, progress as it were by a growing intimacy. And it therefore requires as its indispensable prerequisite a stable body of doctrine and a continuous intellectual tradition."
- **Comparison:** verbatim; the paper embeds mid-sentence (lowercase "its" vs published capital "Its").

## 3. "A philosophy unaware of mystery would not be a philosophy." — VERIFIED_EXACT

- **Paper wording:** "a philosophy unaware of mystery would not be a philosophy"
- **Published (First Lecture, §4, p. 4 in the 1948 printing):** "But philosophy and science also are concerned with mystery, another mystery, the mystery of nature and the mystery of being. A philosophy unaware of mystery would not be a philosophy."
- **Comparison:** verbatim.

## 4. Three intellectual thirsts — PARAPHRASE_CONFIRMED (with two verbatim sub-quotes)

- **Paper wording:** "the thirst for science, the thirst for created wisdom, and the thirst for uncreated wisdom"
- **Published (First Lecture, §6, p. 7 in the 1948 printing):** "6. At this point we must distinguish three kinds of intellectual thirst and three corresponding means of quenching them." — first case: "This is the water of science, useful and bitter."; second case: "This is the water of created wisdom."; third case: "This is the water of uncreated wisdom of which it is written 'Whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst…'"
- **Classification:** PARAPHRASE_CONFIRMED — the paper's three named "thirsts" render the published "three kinds of intellectual thirst" and its three "waters".
- **Sub-quote (VERIFIED_EXACT), paper:** "treating the things of eternal life, the vision of God, as an object of the first thirst"
  **Published (p. 8):** "The climax of spiritual disorder is to confuse the third of these thirsts with the first, by treating the things of eternal life, the vision of God, as an object of the first thirst that namely which belongs to the first case of which I was just speaking, the category of knowledge in which the problem predominates." (OCR shows "thirst that namely which" — likely "thirst, that namely which" in print; the paper's quoted portion up to "first thirst" matches verbatim.)
- **Sub-quote (VERIFIED_EXACT), paper:** "philosophy, metaphysics, wisdom – a category of knowledge in which reverence for the mystery of being is the highest factor – as an object of the first thirst"
  **Published (p. 8):** "But it is also a radical disorder to confuse the second thirst with the first by treating philosophy, metaphysics, wisdom — a category of knowledge in which reverence for the mystery of being is the highest factor — as an object of the first thirst, pre-eminently a problem to answer, a puzzle to solve."

## 5. "too pregnant with intelligibility…" — VERIFIED_EXACT (complete sentence)

- **Paper wording:** "pregnant with intelligibility" … "becoming, potency and above all matter"
- **Published (First Lecture, §4, p. 3 in the 1948 printing; Mentor p. 13):** "The proper object of understanding is being. And being is a mystery, either because it is too pregnant with intelligibility, too pure for our intellect which is the case with spiritual things, or because its nature presents a more or less impenetrable barrier to understanding, a barrier due to the element of non-being in it, which is the case with becoming, potency and above all matter. The mystery we conclude is a fullness of being with which the intellect enters into a vital union and into which it plunges without exhausting it."
- **Note:** completes the partial sentence already recorded in SRC-020 (audio verification); this is the passage behind both the book's "sun" analogy and the paper's sun gloss (§13 below).

## 6. "M. Gabriel Marcel… double aspect" — VERIFIED_EXACT

- **Paper wording:** "making use of terminology borrowed from a contemporary French philosopher, M. Gabriel Marcel, though I am employing it in a completely different sense, we may say that every scientific question presents a double aspect, the one a Mystery, the other a Problem."
- **Published (First Lecture, §4, p. 3 in the 1948 printing; Mentor p. 12):** "Making use of terminology borrowed from a contemporary French philosopher, M. Gabriel Marcel, though I am employing it in a completely different sense, we may say that every scientific question presents a double aspect, the one a Mystery, the other a Problem." Footnote: "See Gabriel Marcel, Position et Approches du mystère ontologique (Le Monde Cassé, Paris, Desclée de Brouwer, 1933)."
- **Comparison:** verbatim (aside from the paper's mid-sentence lowercase "m" in "making").

## 7. "a mystery in regard to the thing… our formulae" — VERIFIED_EXACT

- **Paper wording:** "is a mystery and at the same time a problem, a mystery in regard to the thing, the object as it exists outside the mind, a problem in regard to our formulae."
- **Published (First Lecture, §4, p. 3 in the 1948 printing):** "It is a mystery and at the same time a problem, a mystery in regard to the thing, the object as it exists outside the mind, a problem in regard to our formulae."
- **Comparison:** verbatim (paper begins mid-sentence with "is").

## 8. "supremely mysterious… intelligibility in act" — VERIFIED_EXACT

- **Paper wording:** "the objectivity of the understanding is itself supremely mysterious and the object of knowledge is 'Mystery' reduced to a state of intelligibility in act and of intellection in act."
- **Published (First Lecture, §4, p. 3 in the 1948 printing):** "The objectivity of the understanding is itself supremely mysterious and the object of knowledge is 'Mystery' reduced to a state of intelligibility in act and of intellection in act."
- **Comparison:** verbatim.

## 9. "becomes what is other than itself" — VERIFIED_MINOR_VARIANT

- **Published (First Lecture, §4, p. 3 in the 1948 printing):** "In the act of understanding the intellect becomes what is other than itself, precisely as such. It introduces into itself an inexhaustible (transobjective) reality vitally apprehended as its object."
- **Comparison:** the paper quotes only the middle fragment "becomes what is other than itself" of a longer sentence — fragment quotation, wording itself matches.

## 10. "because there is always some degree of being" — VERIFIED_EXACT (fragment)

- **Published (First Lecture, §5, p. 5 in the 1948 printing; Mentor p. 14):** "The mystery is present because there is always some degree of being, and its depth and thickness must be penetrated."
- **Comparison:** the paper's fragment matches verbatim.

## 11. "least ontological" / "most ontological" / "increasing force and penetration" — VERIFIED_EXACT (fragments)

- **Published (First Lecture, §5, pp. 5–6 in the 1948 printing; Mentor pp. 14–15):**
  - "The problem aspect naturally predominates where knowledge is least ontological, for example, when it is primarily concerned with mental constructions built up around a sensible datum — as in empirical knowledge, and in the sciences of phenomena…"
  - "…dominates where knowledge is most ontological, where it seeks to discover, either intuitively or by analogy, being in itself and the secrets of being…"
  - "Thus the intellect, as its habitus grows more intense, continues, as John of Saint Thomas puts it, to assault its object, the same object, with increasing force and penetration, vehementius et profundius."
- **Comparison:** each fragment matches verbatim.

## 12. "intellectual shock" — VERIFIED_MINOR_VARIANT

- **Paper wording:** the intuition of being is "an intense and highly personal experience that Maritain compared to an intellectual shock."
- **Published (Third Lecture, p. 46 in the 1948 printing; Mentor p. 27):** "we receive at a given moment, as it were the revelation of an intelligible mystery concealed in them. Nor is this revelation, this species of intellectual shock, confined to metaphysicians. It is sometimes given to those who are not metaphysicians…"
- **Comparison:** Maritain's own phrase is "this species of intellectual shock"; the "intense and highly personal experience" framing is not Maritain's in the located passage (likely the paper's or a commentator's wording). Attribute carefully.

## 13. Sun "so bright it cannot be seen" — PARAPHRASE_CONFIRMED (paper's own gloss)

- **Paper wording (no quotation marks):** the first type of mystery "cannot be wholly known due to an abundance of intelligibility, analogous to the sun which is so bright it cannot be seen."
- **Published lines paraphrased:** First Lecture, §4, p. 3 — "too pregnant with intelligibility, too pure for our intellect…" (see §5 above). No sun/owl/bat image found in A Preface to Metaphysics (three OCR scans) or The Degrees of Knowledge (1937 OCR; only St. John of the Cross's star/lamp/sun simile in the mystical-union section).
- **Book comparison:** ch. 2 ("The Sun" section) already uses this analogy from the audio, tied to SRC-020 ("mysteries are pregnant with intelligibility", VERIFIED_MINOR_VARIANT). The paper's gloss and the book's audio-based analogy trace to the same published passage — no conflict.
