## Heard wording (clean transcript)
St. John Henry Newman — when he was a child, he said that he thought the invisible world was more real to him than the visible world. That the visible world was like a shroud placed over us.

## Evidence — verbatim from fetched sources
Apologia, Part I (newmanreader.org): I used to wish the Arabian Tales were true: my imagination ran on unknown influences, on magical powers, and talismans... I thought life might be a dream, or I an Angel, and all this world a deception, my fellow-angels by a playful device concealing themselves from me, and deceiving me with the semblance of a material world. ... It also strengthened my feeling of separation from the visible world, of which I have spoken above.

## Discrepancy / comparison notes
Substance matches the Apologia, Part I. The word 'shroud' does not occur there; the 'veil' image is Newman's from the 'Watching' sermon ('earth and sky are but a veil going between Him and us') — the speaker conflates the two. Newman d. 1890: public domain.
