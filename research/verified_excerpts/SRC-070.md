# SRC-070 — Dante, Paradiso XXXIII.145 (Italian + Longfellow 1867 translation)

- **Heard wording** (first-retreat transcript, evangelization talk; mine T6-S10): "In the incarnation and our configuration to Christ, we have an entrance into the love of Father, Son, Holy Spirit — the love that moves the sun and the stars and the moon." (Mine note: Dante echo; ASR heard "moves the Son"; the added "and the moon" makes it the speaker's own variant — NOT to be printed as a Dante quotation.)
- **Source:** Dante Alighieri, *La Divina Commedia*, Paradiso, Canto XXXIII, line 145 (the final line of the poem). English: Henry Wadsworth Longfellow's translation, *The Divine Comedy*, Vol. III (Paradiso), Boston: Ticknor and Fields, 1867.
- **Edition/translation:** Italian text per it.wikisource (public domain); Longfellow 1867 per en.wikisource (public domain; Longfellow d. 1882)
- **Publisher / year:** Ticknor and Fields, Boston / 1867
- **Locator:** Paradiso XXXIII.145 (final line); Longfellow, Vol. III, Canto XXXIII (final line). Line reference confirmed by the printed line number "144" on the penultimate line of the Italian wikisource text.

## Authoritative excerpts (verbatim from fetched pages)
Italian, final lines (it.wikisource; the page uses typographic apostrophes):
"ma già volgeva il mio disio e 'l velle,
sì come rota ch'igualmente è mossa, 144

l'amor che move il sole e l'altre stelle."

Longfellow 1867, final stanza (en.wikisource):
"Here vigour failed the lofty fantasy:
   But now was turning my desire and will,
   Even as a wheel that equally is moved,

The Love which moves the sun and the other stars."

## Comparison notes
- The heard variant ("the love that moves the sun and the stars and the moon") matches no real Dante line and must not be presented as Dante. Longfellow's actual rendering: "The Love which moves the sun and the other stars." (capital "Love", "which", "the other stars", final period). Italian: "l'amor che move il sole e l'altre stelle."
- If the book prints the real line, use the Longfellow wording above (public domain) or the Italian with reference, cited Paradiso XXXIII.145.
