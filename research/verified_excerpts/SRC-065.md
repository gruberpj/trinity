# SRC-065 — "Every man knocking on the door of a brothel…" (heard as Chesterton; true source Bruce Marshall)

- **Heard wording** (first-retreat transcript, Gift-and-Liturgy talk; mine T4-4; raw ASR): "as one 20th century author said, every man that's knocking on the door of a brothel is seeking in the face of God" (mine reading: "Every man that's knocking on the door of a brothel is seeking the face of God"). The speaker does NOT name the author — he hedges ("as one 20th-century author said").
- **True source (not Chesterton):** Bruce Marshall, *The World, the Flesh, and Father Smith*
- **Edition:** Boston: Houghton Mifflin Company, 1945 (archive.org item `worldfleshfather00mars`, OCR-verified); the American Chesterton Society cites the quotation to this edition at p. 108.
- **Publisher / year:** Houghton Mifflin / 1945 (first published 1944 in the UK)
- **Locator:** p. 108 per the American Chesterton Society (page verified in the Society's note, not pinned from the scan itself); the sentence is dialogue spoken by the novel's title character, Father Smith.
- **Canonical URL:** https://www.chesterton.org/other-quotations/ ; https://archive.org/details/worldfleshfather00mars
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-16
- **Status:** ATTRIBUTION_UNCONFIRMED (as Chesterton — misattribution; true author Bruce Marshall)

## Authoritative excerpt (verbatim)
The American Chesterton Society, "Other Quotations" (fetched 2026-09-16):
"A man knocking on the door of a brothel is knocking for God. Variously ascribed to Chesterton, St. Francis, and St. Augustine, we have discovered that the only documented source of this quotation is the book *The World, The Flesh, and Father Smith* by Bruce Marshall (1945) And the quote is really: '. . .the young man who rings the bell at the brothel is unconsciously looking for God.' (p. 108)"

From the novel's own OCR (archive.org `worldfleshfather00mars`): "…is a substitute for religion and that the young man who rings the bell at the brothel is unconsciously looking for God," Father Smith said.

## Comparison notes
- The heard line is a memory-variant of Bruce Marshall's sentence ("the young man who rings the bell at the brothel is unconsciously looking for God"), not a Chesterton quotation. The speaker's hedge ("as one 20th-century author said") is accurate about the century but the common Chesterton attribution is false.
- Negative corpus check (fetched and searched): the sentence occurs in none of ~45 Project Gutenberg Chesterton texts (including *Heretics*, *Orthodoxy*, *The Defendant*, *The Everlasting Man*, *What's Wrong with the World*). Only unrelated "brothel" mentions exist (e.g., *The Flying Inn*).
- The heard line is NOT the famous "true soldier" line. That line is a different, genuinely Chesterton sentence from his *Illustrated London News* column, 31 December 1910 ("Our Notebook"). The widely circulated wording is a paraphrase: "The true soldier fights not because he hates what is in front of him, but because he loves what is behind him." Chesterton's original formulation, verified in the ILN scan OCR (archive.org `sim_illustrated-london-news_1910-12-31_137_3741`) and the ACS quotations page: "A real soldier does not fight because he has something that he hates in front of him. He fights because he has something that he loves behind his back." (ACS note: "This popular version is actually a paraphrase. Chesterton's original formulation is, 'A real soldier does not fight because he has something that he hates in front of him. He fights because he has something that he loves behind his back.' – Ed." — https://www.chesterton.org/quotations/war-and-politics/)
- Recommendation for the book: if the brothel line is used, attribute to Bruce Marshall (*The World, the Flesh, and Father Smith*, 1945, p. 108) — not Chesterton — and preserve the speaker's hedge, or drop the quotation.
