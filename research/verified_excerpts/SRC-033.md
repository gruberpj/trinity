## Heard wording (clean transcript)
We remember the dream of St. Augustine, walking along a beach and finding a boy, digging a hole, taking a pail and going to the ocean and dumping ocean water into that hole... 'I'm pouring the ocean into this hole.' ... And that is also the case, the boy says, when you try to understand God as one in three.

## Evidence — verbatim from fetched sources
Caxton's Golden Legend (1483), 'The Life of St. Austin' (Caxton's own addition): It was so that this glorious doctor... on a time as he went by the sea-side in Africa, studying on the Trinity, he found by the sea-side a little child which had made a little pit in the sand, and in his hand a little spoon. And with the spoon he took out water of the large sea and poured it into the pit. ... 'Yes, forsooth,' said he, 'I shall lightlier and sooner draw all the water of the sea and bring it into this pit than thou shalt bring the mystery of the Trinity and his divinity into thy little understanding...' And therewith the child vanished away. Caxton's caveat: howbeit I find it not in the legend, mine exemplar, neither in English, French, ne in Latin.

## Discrepancy / comparison notes
Traditional LEGEND, not history: not in Augustine's own writings or early vitae; not in Voragine's Legenda Aurea (c. 1270); earliest written attestation located = Caxton's 1483 Golden Legend, added by Caxton from a painting at Antwerp (story circulating by the 15th c.). The speaker retells it as a story, not a quotation. Any book use must label it as legend/tradition.
