## 1. Mystery definition — VERIFIED_EXACT

- **Paper wording:** "a mystery is a problem which encroaches upon its own data, invading them, as it were, and thereby transcending itself as a simple problem"
- **Published (The Philosophy of Existence, trans. Harari, Harvill 1948, "On the Ontological Mystery", p. ~8, scan leaf 20):** "At this point we can begin to define the distinction between mystery and problem. A mystery is a problem which encroaches upon its own data, invading them, as it were, and thereby transcending itself as a simple problem. A set of examples will help us to grasp the content of this definition."
- **Variant (Being and Having, trans. Farrer, Dacre Press 1949, p. 171):** "A mystery is a problem which encroaches upon its own data and invades them, and so is transcended qua problem." Also, Metaphysical Diary, entry of Feb. 2nd: "We must say that mystery is a problem that encroaches upon the intrinsic conditions of its own possibility (and not upon its data)."
- **Comparison:** the paper matches the Harvill 1948 wording word for word (including "invading them, as it were, and thereby transcending itself as a simple problem"); the Being and Having wording differs and would not match the paper.

## 2. Problem "placed in front of me, blocking my way" — PARAPHRASE_CONFIRMED

- **Paper wording:** a problem is something that "is placed in front of me, blocking my way"
- **Published (Being and Having, p. 100, section "Distinguish between the Mysterious and the Problematic"):** "A problem is something met with which bars my passage. It is before me in its entirety."
- **Comparison:** the paper's phrasing is its own rendering of the published sentence, not a verbatim quotation.

## 3. Technical problems — VERIFIED_EXACT

- **Paper wording:** technical problems, which "can not be solved without a measure of pre-established theoretical knowledge"
- **Published (The Philosophy of Existence, "On the Ontological Mystery", p. ~4, scan leaf 16):** "These theoretical and technical questions are interdependent, for the theoretical problems arise out of the different techniques while the technical problems can not be solved without a measure of pre-established theoretical knowledge."
- **Comparison:** verbatim, including "can not" as two words in both.

## 4. Recollection — VERIFIED_EXACT

- **Paper wording:** recollection is "the act whereby I re-collect myself as a unity"
- **Published (The Philosophy of Existence, "On the Ontological Mystery", p. ~12, scan leaf 24):** "It should be noted that recollection, which has received little enough attention from pure philosophers, is very difficult to define—if only because it transcends the dualism of being and action or, more correctly, because it reconciles in itself these two aspects of the antinomy. The word means what it says—the act whereby I re-collect myself as a unity; but this hold, this grasp upon myself is also relaxation and abandon."
- **Comparison:** verbatim. Source is The Philosophy of Existence (not Creative Fidelity, the earlier guess).

## 5. "can be neither analysed nor reconstituted out of precedent elements" — VERIFIED_EXACT (fragment; application drift flagged)

- **Paper wording:** Mysteries "can be neither analysed nor reconstituted out of precedent elements"
- **Published (The Philosophy of Existence, p. ~9, scan leaf 21):** "expressed by such phrases as I have a body, I make use of my body, I feel my body, etc., can be neither analysed nor reconstituted out of precedent elements. It is not only data, I would say that it is the basis of data, in the sense of being my own presence to myself, a presence of which the act of self-consciousness is, in the last analysis, only an inadequate symbol."
- **Comparison:** fragment matches verbatim, but the published sentence concerns the body ("I have a body…"), while the paper applies it to mysteries generally — flag for human review.

## 6. "derived from experience… meta-problematical and of mystery" — VERIFIED_MINOR_VARIANT

- **Paper wording:** "for content is, when all is said and done, derived from experience; whereas, it is only by a way of liberation and detachment from experience that we can possibly rise to the level of the meta-problematical and of mystery."
- **Published (The Philosophy of Existence, p. ~12, scan leaf 24):** "As for the term content of thought which figured in the objection, it is deceptive in the highest degree. For content is, when all is said and done, derived from experience; whereas it is only by a way of liberation and detachment from experience that we can possibly rise to the level of the meta-problematical and of mystery. This liberation must be real; this detachment must be real; they must not be an abstraction, that is to say a fiction recognised as such."
- **Comparison:** sole difference is the paper's comma after "whereas" (published: "whereas it is only").

## 7. "has its basis in resentment… integrity of the real" — VERIFIED_EXACT

- **Paper wording:** "has its basis in resentment, that is to say, in passion, and at bottom it corresponds to a violent attack directed against a sort of integrity of the real"
- **Published (Man Against Mass Society, trans. Fraser, Part One, ch. III "The Spirit of Abstraction, as a Factor Making for War", scan leaf 172):** "every depreciatory reduction of this sort has its basis in resentment, that is to say, in passion, and at bottom it corresponds to a violent attack directed against a sort of integrity of the real, an integrity to which only a resolutely concrete mode of thinking can hope to do justice."
- **Comparison:** verbatim. Note the paper's framing sentence says "derogatory reduction"; the published text says "depreciatory reduction".

## 8. "profoundly linked to a tendency to conceptual dissociation" — VERIFIED_MINOR_VARIANT (subject mismatch)

- **Paper wording:** the spirit of abstraction is the "contempt for the concrete conditions of abstract thinking," being "profoundly linked to a tendency to conceptual dissociation."
- **Published (Man Against Mass Society, ch. III, scan leaf 174):** "The fact rather is, or so it seems to me, that the element of resentment in human nature is profoundly linked to a tendency to conceptual dissociation — in this, lying at the opposite pole to the element of admiration."
- **Comparison:** the quoted wording matches, but the published subject is "the element of resentment in human nature", not "the spirit of abstraction" as the paper's syntax implies. Human review recommended.

## 9. "mystery of knowledge which is of the ontological order" — VERIFIED_MINOR_VARIANT

- **Paper wording:** Maritain introduced Marcel to the "mystery of knowledge which is of the ontological order"
- **Published (Being and Having, p. 100):** "The Mysterious and Ontological are identical. There is a mystery of knowledge which belongs to the ontological order (as Maritain saw) but the epistemologist does not know this, makes a point of ignoring it, and turns it into a problem."
- **Comparison:** paper: "of the ontological order"; published: "belongs to the ontological order". The Maritain credit "(as Maritain saw)" is present in the published text.
- **Related (unverified):** the paper's other half of this attribution — through Maritain "that I was led to make the fundamental distinction" — was NOT located in Being and Having or The Philosophy of Existence OCR (the only Maritain mention in The Philosophy of Existence is the Louvain "Problem of Christian Philosophy" quotation, p. ~15, scan leaf 27). ATTRIBUTION_UNCONFIRMED.
