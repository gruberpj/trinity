## Authoritative excerpts (OCR of the three books, copied verbatim from the search-inside API; the API's match-highlight markers omitted)
1. *Good News About Sex and Marriage* (2000), p. 198: "I once heard it said that intimacy means 'into-me-see.' If husband and wife are intentionally hiding their faces from each other during sex, then something's wrong. They should be …" (sentence continuation beyond the OCR window; "some-thing's" hyphenation is an OCR line-break artifact.)
2. Revised edition (2004), p. 206: "I once heard it said that intimacy means 'into-me-see.' If husband and wife are afraid to show their faces to each other during sex, then something's wrong. They should be able to …"
3. *Theology of the Body Explained* (2003), p. 558: "I once heard the word 'intimacy' defined as 'into-me-see.' This explains well that experience of original nakedness. There is no fear in being seen externally because there is no fear of being seen internally."

## Comparison notes
- West demonstrably uses and popularizes the wordplay — it appears in three of his books (2000–2004), so the speaker's attribution to West as its source for Catholic audiences is confirmed.
- Coinage caveat: in all three books West introduces it with an explicit hedge — "I once heard it said…" / "I once heard the word 'intimacy' defined as…". No fetched source shows West naming anyone else or claiming to have coined it. So the Q&A claim "Did you make it up yourself? — Christopher West did" is NOT supported; the mine's own note ("Verify audio before printing the name") still applies, and the printed book should not assert West coined the phrase.
- The heard phrase is a definition-wordplay, not a quotation of West — no verbatim match expected or claimed.
- The wordplay is a folk/false etymology (intimacy derives from Latin *intimus*); the phrase is extremely widespread (hundreds of books in the full-text index contain it). No clean fetched source stating the *intimus* derivation verbatim was obtained.
